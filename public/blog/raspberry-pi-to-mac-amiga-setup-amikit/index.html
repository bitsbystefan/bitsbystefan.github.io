From Raspberry Pi to Mac: Simplifying My Amiga Setup with AmiKit X13.1

2026-10-18

JIT compilation support has been available in Amiberry since version 8.0.0, but I started my macOS setup with Amiberry 8.3.0 on an Apple Silicon Mac mini with an M2 chip. This combination made me reconsider my existing Amiga setup.

Originally, I deliberately kept AmiKit X13.1 on a dedicated Raspberry Pi. I liked having a separate machine for retro computing, independent of my everyday environment. It was a perfectly reasonable setup, and the Raspberry Pi could always find another purpose down the road.

However, running AmiKit X13.1 on macOS, with Amiberry taking advantage of JIT compilation on Apple Silicon, offers a compelling alternative. Instead of maintaining a separate device, I can run my Amiga environment directly on the Mac mini I already use. Performance is excellent, and after some configuration and fine-tuning, the experience feels just right.

The final confirmation came during a Friday evening gaming session. A round of Deluxe Galaga, paired with my new 8BitDo Pro 2 controller, demonstrated how well the setup works in practice. Even my choice of display has evolved along the way, with my secondary Full HD monitor (usually used for screen sharing) proving more comfortable for this particular use than my UWQHD display.

The important point is not simply that I moved an emulator from one platform to another. The combination of software improvements and Apple Silicon performance made a simpler overall architecture practical. What once justified a separate Raspberry Pi no longer requires dedicated hardware, without sacrificing the experience I wanted.

The Raspberry Pi will eventually find another job. For now, AmiKit X13.1 on the M2 Mac mini has become my preferred Amiga environment.
